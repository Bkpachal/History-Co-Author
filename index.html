What Is History Co-Author?

History Co-Author is an AI-assisted framework created for nonfiction authors and history enthusiasts who want assistance researching and writing narrative history books. It treats research, planning, manuscript development, continuity, and supporting documentation as connected stages of a larger publishing project.

Users begin by selecting a historical subject, providing relevant source files, and answering questions about their proposed book. According to the supplied demonstration, the workflow involves three framework files and 18 questions. The system then helps organize research and create planning documents that guide manuscript development.

The system is designed to work with Claude and ChatGPT, but it does not eliminate the need for historical research or editorial judgment. Authors must independently verify quotations, sources, dates, interpretations, and factual statements before publishing their work.

How Does History Co-Author Work?

History Co-Author follows a structured Plan → Research → Write → Review → Publish methodology. It combines source material, planning documents, AI-assisted research, chapter development, and supporting records into a connected nonfiction writing workflow.

1

Step 1: Choose Your Historical Subject

Begin by identifying the historical topic you want to investigate. It could involve a military campaign, historical figure, political event, family history, cultural development, expedition, invention, or social movement.

Define the subject and research direction before beginning manuscript development.

2

Step 2: Upload Research Files and Answer Questions

The demonstrated workflow involves providing three framework files inside a Claude or ChatGPT project and answering 18 questions about the proposed book.

You can bring existing research materials into the process, giving the system a foundation for organizing information and developing the project.

3

Step 3: Build the Research Foundation

The framework is designed to help investigate sources, evaluate reliability, compare conflicting historical accounts, and examine eyewitness material and official records.

The objective is to create an organized research record that connects important historical claims with supporting evidence.

4

Step 4: Create Your Book Structure

The workflow identifies several planning documents, including a Book Bible, Research Bible, People, Places and Entities document, and Chapter Outline.

These resources help organize the manuscript's overall structure, historical context, characters, and chapter progression before substantial writing begins.

5

Step 5: Develop Chapters Sequentially

Rather than requesting an entire book in one generation, the system supports chapter-by-chapter writing using the research record, framework, and Book State.

This approach is intended to help maintain consistency across characters, dates, locations, events, and historical details as the manuscript grows.

6

Step 6: Prepare Supporting Documentation

After developing the manuscript, the workflow can help prepare supporting materials such as a cast of characters, notes on names, source notes, chapter notes, and a selected bibliography.

These materials help authors organize the research behind the book and prepare for the final editing and publishing process. All references should still be checked against original sources.

What Makes the Narrative History Approach Different?

Generic AI prompts often produce broad historical summaries that communicate basic information without necessarily developing an engaging narrative. A structured narrative history approach considers the people, circumstances, decisions, conflicts, chronology, and evidence surrounding an event.

For example, instead of beginning with a generic historical statement such as:

The campaign began in the autumn of 1790.

A narrative approach may explore the people involved, the political and military circumstances, the decisions that shaped the campaign, and the available historical evidence. It attempts to provide readers with a clearer understanding of how events unfolded rather than simply presenting a list of dates.

The important distinction is that narrative history makes documented events more readable without inventing dialogue, fictional scenes, or unsupported historical details. Evidence remains the foundation of the finished manuscript.
